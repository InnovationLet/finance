On a normal Tuesday, roughly $60 billion of stablecoin value changes hands, and almost none of it buys anything. Most of that traffic is exchange settlement, treasury rebalancing, and cross-border transfers between desks that never touch a consumer checkout. The plumbing works, but it works in a narrower band than the headline numbers suggest.

## Where the volume actually clears

Three corridors account for most real payment flow: dollar transfers into Latin American and Southeast Asian bank accounts, exchange-to-exchange settlement, and corporate treasury moves between crypto-native firms. A wire from a US company to a Philippine supplier still ends in pesos at a local bank. The stablecoin leg is the fast part; the off-ramp is where delays and fees concentrate.

Corridor concentration matters because it determines what breaks first. When one exchange gates redemptions or one off-ramp partner suspends withdrawals, the entire route stalls. The token keeps trading at $1.00 on-screen while the actual cash-out takes three days.

## Weekend and holiday gaps

Bank rails close. Stablecoin rails don't. That gap cuts both ways. A $40 million transfer that lands Saturday afternoon sits untouched until Monday morning at the receiving bank, which means someone is holding counterparty risk over two nights with no conventional hedge available.

Treasury desks have started treating this as a known exposure rather than a surprise. They pre-fund, they split transfers across windows, and they keep a buffer at the destination. None of that is glamorous, and all of it exists because the token moved faster than the money underneath it.

## The redemption risk nobody prices

Here's the part that gets glossed over: a stablecoin is only as good as the issuer's redemption queue under stress. Reserve disclosures have improved, but a disclosure is not a guarantee of same-day conversion at par when a million holders ask at once.

Watch two signals. First, the spread between the token's market price and peg on low-liquidity venues, not the majors. Second, the issuer's disclosure cadence during a stress event. Silence during a depeg is itself information.

**Your takeaway:** if you're using stablecoins for actual payments, map your exit before you need it. Confirm which off-ramp you'll use, what hours it operates, and what happens to your settlement if it's down for 72 hours. The token is the easy part.
