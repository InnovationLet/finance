A $100 card transaction typically generates about $1.90 in interchange. After network fees, fraud losses, issuer processing, and rewards, the company that issued the card might keep $0.40. That's the margin a whole product team is building on.

## Interchange is the business model

Strip away the branding and a lot of fintech payment products are a thin layer over an interchange stream. Neobanks, cash-back cards, expense platforms with embedded cards, they're all renting the same revenue source. When network rules or regulatory caps move that number, entire strategies get rewritten overnight.

This is why two seemingly identical products can perform completely differently. One negotiated a favorable issuer BIN sponsor and a rewards deal that leaves room. The other is paying a processor a per-transaction fee that eats the spread. Same consumer feature, opposite unit economics.

Watch interchange per transaction across quarters, not total payment volume. Volume is vanity when the spread is compressing.

## Where the margin leaks

- **Fraud and disputes.** Card-not-present fraud keeps climbing, and liability often sits with the fintech, not the network.
- **Rewards.** A 2% cash-back offer funds itself only if average interchange clears that plus processing costs. Subprime or low-spend cohorts break the math.
- **Processing and BIN sponsorship.** Per-transaction fees vary widely, and newer entrants often pay the highest rates.
- **Customer acquisition.** Sign-up bonuses and referral credits can take 12 to 18 months of interchange to pay back.

Any one of these running hot turns a profitable cohort into a subsidized one.

## The winners price the cohort, not the product

The fintechs holding durable payment margins do one unglamorous thing well: they segment spend behavior at onboarding. They know which users transact at high-interchange merchants, which ones carry balances, and which ones will never cover their own rewards cost. Then they design the product around that mix instead of around a marketing hook.

**Your takeaway:** before using or investing in any fintech payment product, find its interchange dependency. Ask what happens to the offer if interchange drops 20 basis points. If the answer is vague or the team can't explain their fraud loss rate, you're looking at a subsidy, not a business.
